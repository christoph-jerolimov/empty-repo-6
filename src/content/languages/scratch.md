## Hello, World!

Scratch programs are assembled from drag-and-drop blocks rather than typed. The equivalent script, in the text notation used by the Scratch community, is:

```text
when green flag clicked
say [Hello, World!]
```

Snapping a "when green flag clicked" hat block onto a "say" block from the Looks palette makes the sprite display the greeting in a speech bubble.

## About

In Scratch, programs are built by snapping together colour-coded blocks that only fit when the combination is syntactically valid, which removes syntax errors entirely. Scripts are attached to sprites on a stage and respond to events such as clicks, key presses and messages, running concurrently. Despite its simplicity it has variables, lists, loops, conditionals, custom blocks (procedures), cloning and broadcasting, and children use it to make games, animations and interactive stories which they share on the Scratch website, one of the largest online creative communities for young people.

## History

Mitchel Resnick's Lifelong Kindergarten group at the MIT Media Lab, building on the ideas of Seymour Papert's Logo and on earlier work with LEGO Mindstorms and the Computer Clubhouse network, released Scratch in May 2007. Scratch 2.0 (2013) moved to the browser, and Scratch 3.0 (2019), rebuilt in JavaScript with Google's Blockly collaboration, added extensions for hardware, translation and text-to-speech. The Scratch Foundation was established in 2013, and the language's block-based approach has been adopted by App Inventor, micro:bit MakeCode, Code.org and many other teaching platforms.
