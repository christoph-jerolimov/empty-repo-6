## Hello, World!

```text
print [Hello, World!]
```

The turtle version, which is how most learners first met the language:

```text
repeat 4 [forward 100 right 90]
```

## About

Logo is a dialect of Lisp with a friendlier syntax, designed around the idea that children learn best by building and debugging things themselves. Its most famous feature is the turtle, originally a floor robot and later an on-screen cursor, which is driven with commands such as `forward` and `right` to draw geometry. Underneath, Logo is a full functional language with lists, recursion and first-class procedures, and it was used to teach everything from arithmetic to music and linguistics.

## History

Logo was created in 1967 at Bolt, Beranek and Newman in Cambridge, Massachusetts, by Wally Feurzeig, Seymour Papert and Cynthia Solomon. Papert, who had worked with Jean Piaget, took the language to the MIT AI Lab where turtle graphics was developed, and his 1980 book *Mindstorms* made constructionist learning a movement. Versions for the Apple II, Atari and Commodore machines brought Logo into schools worldwide in the 1980s. Its influence continues in StarLogo and NetLogo, in LEGO's Mindstorms robotics kits named after Papert's book, and in Scratch, which was created by Papert's students at MIT.
