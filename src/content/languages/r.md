## About

R is built around vectors, data frames and formulas, with a rich environment for fitting statistical models, running tests and producing publication-quality graphics with base graphics, lattice or ggplot2. It is a functional language with lazy evaluation of arguments, lexical scoping and several object systems (S3, S4, R5 and R7), and its metaprogramming facilities enable the "tidy evaluation" style of the tidyverse. More than 20,000 CRAN packages cover almost every statistical technique ever published, which keeps R central in academia, pharmaceuticals, finance and public-health work.

## History

Ross Ihaka and Robert Gentleman at the University of Auckland began R in 1991 as an implementation of the S language, developed at Bell Labs by John Chambers from 1976, with scoping borrowed from Scheme. It was announced publicly in 1993 and released under the GPL in 1995, and the R Core Team was formed in 1997 together with CRAN. R 1.0.0 shipped in 2000. The RStudio IDE (2011) and Hadley Wickham's tidyverse packages broadened its appeal enormously during the 2010s data-science boom, and R 4.0 (2020) continued steady annual improvements.
