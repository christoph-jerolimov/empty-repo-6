## About

Ruby blends Smalltalk's pure object model, Perl's text-processing convenience and Lisp's flexibility into a syntax designed to feel natural to humans. Everything, including integers and `nil`, is an object; blocks and iterators replace most explicit loops; and open classes, `method_missing` and powerful reflection make it a favourite for building internal domain-specific languages. The Ruby on Rails web framework (2004) popularised convention-over-configuration and shaped a generation of web frameworks, and the language remains the backbone of GitHub, Shopify, Stripe's early systems and much of the startup world.

## History

Yukihiro Matsumoto began Ruby in February 1993 in Japan and released it publicly in December 1995, aiming for a scripting language "more powerful than Perl and more object-oriented than Python". The English-language *Programming Ruby* ("Pickaxe book", 2001) spread it beyond Japan, and David Heinemeier Hansson's Rails made it a global phenomenon from 2005. Ruby 1.9 (2007) introduced a new bytecode VM, Ruby 2.0 (2013) and the 2012 ISO standard marked maturity, and Ruby 3.0 (2020) delivered on "Ruby 3x3" performance goals with a JIT compiler and added static type signatures (RBS) and the Ractor concurrency model.
